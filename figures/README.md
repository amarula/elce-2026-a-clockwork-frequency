# Figure sources

Every chart in `../assets/*.png` is generated by the scripts here. Regenerating
is byte-reproducible: run a script and it overwrites its own asset in place.

```sh
python3 -m venv --system-site-packages ~/.venvs/ssc-figs
~/.venvs/ssc-figs/bin/pip install matplotlib numpy scipy
cd figures
for f in plot_0*.py; do ~/.venvs/ssc-figs/bin/python "$f"; done   # regenerates every chart
```

The venv lives outside the repo (`~/.venvs/ssc-figs`) so it survives a clean
checkout and needs no `.gitignore` entry. Matplotlib is not in the system
Python, so a bare `python3 plot_*.py` fails with `ModuleNotFoundError`.

## Colour grammar

One meaning per role, declared once in `plot_style.py`. Don't re-declare hex
values in individual scripts.

| Colour | Constant | Means |
|---|---|---|
| blue `#0000EE` | `BLUE` | the clock's spectrum, **SSC off** |
| orange `#E69F00` | `ORANGE` | the clock's spectrum, **SSC on** |
| red `#CC0000` | `RED` | the emission limit, and the ✘ fail mark |
| green `#1C7A1C` | `GREEN` | a victim device's band, the ✔ mark, the PASS badge |
| magenta `#8E1A6B` | `MAGENTA` | the newly-overlapped sliver (zoom chart only) |
| gray `#444444` | `GRAY` | measurement callouts that assert no verdict |

Red is reserved for *the limit and failure*, so the fix is never drawn in it —
painting SSC red made the remedy look like the problem. Green is reserved for
*compliance verdicts*, a different claim from "this clock is spread".

### Why blue → orange, and not blue → green

**The speaker is colour-blind on the blue–green axis, so "green = safe" is not
an available channel.** That the spread clock is compliant is carried by
*position* (the band sits under the limit line) and by the ✔ / `EMC: PASS`
badge — never by the band's hue. Do not "fix" the palette by making SSC green
or teal to make it look reassuring.

Blue vs orange is the Okabe-Ito CVD-safe contrast, and it is the pair the whole
before/after argument rests on, so it is the pair that has to survive every
deficiency. ΔE between the two, simulated:

| | normal | protan | deutan | tritan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| blue vs orange | 191 | 164 | 179 | **78** |
| blue vs teal *(rejected)* | 143 | 92 | 96 | **36** |

Teal was tried first and had to be thrown out: under tritanopia it collapses
into *both* `BLUE` and `GREEN` at once. `MAGENTA` replaced the old amber sliver
for the same reason — amber and orange are nearly the same colour.

Two weak pairs remain, both pre-existing and both mitigated by shape rather
than hue: under protan/deutan `RED` and `GREEN` converge (ΔE 18), so the ✔/✘
glyphs and the `FAIL`/`PASS` wording — not the colour — carry the verdict; and
under tritan the emission-limit line goes pink like the orange bands, separated
by being a thin dashed horizontal rule against solid vertical blocks.

**Re-check after any palette change** — don't eyeball it:

```sh
npx slidev export --format png --output /tmp/vcheck
cd figures && ~/.venvs/ssc-figs/bin/python cvd_check.py
```

That writes `../cvd-check-tritan.png` and `../cvd-check-deutan.png`: slides 9–12
as they actually look under each deficiency.

## Measurements: one arrow idiom

Every measured span in the deck goes through `plot_style.dimension()`: **tick
caps at both ends and a double-headed arrow between them**, the ISO/ASME
dimension line. Arrows placed *outside* a span pointing inwards — which the
victim bands used to use — are the drawing convention's fallback for spans too
narrow to hold their own arrowheads. Nothing here is that narrow, and having
both forms in one deck made it look like two different notations for one thing.

Caps and heads are drawn via `arrowstyle`, i.e. in display space, so they come
out the same visual size on a 620 MHz axis and on a 2 MHz one with no per-chart
tuning. `dimension_line()` in `plot_05_victim_band` wraps it for victim bands,
adding the ✔/✘ label above and the boundary frequencies below; those numbers sit
just *outside* their own tick, because centred under it they collided as soon as
a span got short (Device B printed "100.2118").

The one variant is `runs_off_to=`: when the far edge is outside the window, the
dimension gets one cap and a single trailing arrowhead instead of a false second
edge. Slide 12 needs it — Device B ends at 118 MHz, far off a 2 MHz window.

Leader lines (`->` pointing *at* a thing, as on slides 5–7) are a different
idiom and stay as they are: they identify, they don't measure.

### Fill and outline are one colour

A filled band is its role's colour at `FILL_ALPHA` (0.45), outlined in the
**same hex** at full strength — the regular and bold of one colour, never two
colours. The alpha used to be 0.25, which washed the fill out until it read as
a second, unrelated colour against its own outline. On the zoom chart the
orange fill *stops* where the magenta sliver starts rather than running
underneath it: two translucent fills stacked blend into a third colour that
means nothing. The outline still spans the whole band, so it reads as one
object with an inset region.

Slides 9 → 11 are the before/after pair and blue → orange is the whole visual
argument; slide 10 establishes the legend for it immediately beforehand.

## Single source of truth

`plot_05_victim_band.py` holds the SSC parameters that every other chart derives
from — change them there and nothing can drift:

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `SPREAD_DEPTH_PCT` | `0.02` | ±2 % — the **conceptual** slides' depth, see below. |
| `RBW_MHZ` | `0.15` | Measurement resolution bandwidth. |
| `THRESHOLD` | `-12` | Emission limit. **Pinned by the depth** — see below. |
| `DEVICE_B` | `(101, 118)` | Its lower edge must sit inside the spread band. |
| `FLOOR` / `YMAX` | `-60` / `30` | Axis range shared by the two full-spectrum charts. |

`spread_half_width(f)` and `reduction_db(f)` are the two functions the rest of
the deck calls. The peak reduction is **not** a hand-picked number: it is
`10*log10(spread bandwidth / RBW)`, which is why higher harmonics gain more —
their absolute deviation scales with the harmonic order.

### Two depths, on purpose

There is **no single deck-wide depth**, and that is a deliberate decision rather
than drift:

| | depth | why |
|---|---|---|
| slides 9–12 (`plot_05*`, `plot_07`) | **±2 %** | chosen for legibility |
| slide 14 (`plot_08.DEPTH_PCT`) | **±1 %** | chosen for realism |

Slides 9–12 argue about *what spreading does* — a spike becomes a band, the band
clears the limit, the band reaches a neighbour — and none of them names a number
on screen. At a realistic depth every one of those pictures collapses: on a
620 MHz axis the fundamental's band would be a 1 MHz hairline, and the newly
overlapped sliver on slide 12 would need the window closed to 2 MHz to be
visible at all. So they are drawn deep enough to read.

Slide 14 is the one that puts the parameters on screen, so it carries a value a
real part would take. ±1 % sits inside what configurable clock generators offer.
It is not the serial standards' ±0.5 % because the profile comparison in row 1
needs the band to be several resolution bandwidths wide to survive — see the
table below.

Both go through the same `spread_half_width(f, depth_pct)` and
`reduction_db(f, depth_pct)`, so there is still **one formula** behind every
number; only the illustrative setting differs. Slide 11's speaker notes say out
loud that the drawn spread is exaggerated, so the talk never contradicts itself.

### What the depth pins

Whatever depth a chart uses, these move with it:

- **`THRESHOLD`** must sit above the post-SSC fundamental or slide 11 never
  reaches `EMC: PASS`. At ±2 % that is −14.26 dB, so −12 works and leaves the
  3rd harmonic failing too — two red crosses. Below about ±0.7 % no threshold can
  have the 3rd harmonic fail *before* and the fundamental pass *after*, and
  slide 9 drops to a single failing peak.
- **`DEVICE_B`'s lower edge** has to sit inside the spread band or slide 12 has
  nothing to show. At ±2 % the band reaches 102, hence 101.
- **Every chart's frequency window.**
- **The profile penalty**, because it depends on band/RBW rather than on depth
  directly — the wider the band relative to the filter, the more of the
  sinusoid's horns survive:

  | depth | band | band/RBW | triangular | sinusoidal | penalty |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | ±0.5 % | 1 MHz | 6.7 | −8.24 | −7.03 | 1.21 dB |
  | **±1 %** | 2 MHz | 13.3 | **−11.25** | **−8.59** | **2.66 dB** |
  | ±2 % | 4 MHz | 26.7 | −14.26 | −10.13 | 4.13 dB |

  This is why slide 14 uses ±1 % rather than the standards' ±0.5 %: at 0.5 % the
  filter smooths the horns down to about a decibel and row 1 stops being an
  argument. On higher harmonics the band is n times wider and the penalty grows
  again, which the speaker notes mention.

## plot_08 — the parameter-comparison layout

One row per configuration, three cells each: the parameter list, the time
domain, the resulting spectrum. `render()` takes a list of configs and
`changed_key()` **raises if a row moves more than one knob** — the slide's whole
premise is that each row is a controlled experiment, so the code enforces it.
The knob that changed is boxed automatically in that row's list.

The chain on the slide, at ±1 % depth and a fixed 5 kHz rate:

| # | profile | type | band | peak | shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | sinusoidal | center | 99–101 | −8.59 dB | horns from uneven dwell |
| 2 | triangular | center | 99–101 | **−11.25 dB** | flat band is optimal, +2.7 dB |
| 3 | triangular | down | 98–100 | −11.25 dB | sits under nominal, peak unchanged |
| 4 | triangular | up | 100–102 | −11.25 dB | mirror image, peak unchanged |

Sinusoidal leads deliberately so rows 2–4 all carry the profile that ships.
**Rows 2–4 repeating one number to the decimal is the content, not a
copy-paste slip**: of the four knobs only profile (and depth) buy decibels.
In the time cells the payoff is where the orange trace sits against the blue
nominal line — across, under, over — so don't reorder those three rows.

### On the rate, and why it is not a row

`RATE_KHZ = 5.0`. Held fixed on purpose. Two things rule out a rate row:

- Inside the range real parts offer, **the rate changes nothing measurable**:
  3, 30, 33, 50 and 100 kHz all give −11.25 dB for a triangular sweep. The first
  visible departure is ~150 kHz (0.24 dB); it only bites past ~200 kHz.
- The values that *do* break the band into resolved sidebands — several hundred
  kHz — are values no part will take. A draft used **500 kHz** for exactly that
  picture (−9.30 dB, a 5 dB give-back). It was cut: a real mechanism illustrated
  with a fictional number. If you want that picture back it belongs on its own
  slide, labelled as out of range. Do not quietly reinstate it here.

5 kHz is the order of magnitude of SSC built into SoC PLLs (STM32, i.MX, TI
DPLLs), which is the case this talk is about — as opposed to the 30–33 kHz the
serial standards specify for links whose receiver tracks the wander with a CDR.
It also gives a round 200 µs period. `T_WINDOW` holds ~3 cycles of it.

Both chart cells share the same frequency window (`F_LO`..`F_HI`), vertical on
the left and horizontal on the right, so the sweep's excursion and the band are
visibly the same span rotated ninety degrees. **Keep those two ranges equal**;
that correspondence is the reason the cells sit side by side. `T_WINDOW` is
likewise fixed across rows — drawing a constant number of cycles instead would
make a change of rate invisible in the time domain, which is most of row 3.

Panels carry no "Time Domain"/"Frequency Domain" titles: the axes say it, the
speaker says it, and four rows need the vertical space.

### How the spectrum is computed

Not a drawn rectangle, and not an FM simulation. Frequency modulation puts the
carrier's power into a **comb of sidebands spaced by the modulation rate**, and
for a large modulation index the envelope of that comb is the sweep's dwell
distribution — the time spent per unit frequency. `spectrum_db` builds exactly
that and passes it through the analyser's resolution filter. Both interesting
knobs fall out of the one expression:

- **profile sets the envelope**, via `dwell_cdf`. Triangular sweeps at a constant
  rate → uniform dwell → flat band. A sinusoid slows to a stop at its turning
  points and lingers → the **arcsine law**, whose density *diverges* at the band
  edges. That is the physical origin of the horns.
- **rate sets the line spacing.** While the spacing stays well inside the RBW the
  filter merges the lines and the rate is irrelevant to the peak. Push it past
  the RBW and the analyser resolves individual lines — and a resolved line is a
  narrow peak again. That is the whole of row 3, and it is why the quasi-static
  model alone (which is rate-blind) could not draw this slide.

Two implementation points that are not free choices:

- Line powers come from the **CDF**, never from sampling the density: the arcsine
  density is infinite at the band edges, its integral is not.
- The filter is Gaussian with `FWHM = RBW`, the usual instrument convention,
  normalised so a flat dwell distribution lands at exactly `RBW/(2·half_width)`.
  A slow triangular sweep therefore reproduces `reduction_db()` to the fourth
  decimal, and there is an `assert` at the bottom of the file that fails if that
  ever drifts. **The filter shape is not cosmetic**: because the arcsine density
  diverges, the horn height is set entirely by it — a hard rectangular RBW window
  keeps ~5 dB of horn, this Gaussian keeps ~4 dB.

What this model does *not* do: Bessel ripple. A real FM spectrum has it, and the
quasi-static envelope slightly overstates the sinusoid's horns. Quote these
decibels as the model's, not a measurement's — the mechanisms and the signs are
right. (An older simulator, since deleted, put the sinusoid's penalty nearer
3 dB than 4.)
